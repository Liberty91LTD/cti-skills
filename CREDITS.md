# Credits

This pack codifies established Cyber Threat Intelligence tradecraft into composable agent skills. It rests on decades of public scholarship, open standards, free training material from CTI educators, and vendor research that the community publishes openly. We've named the people and organisations whose work shaped how the skills here are built — for the per-skill breakdown see the [Per-skill attribution](#per-skill-attribution) table at the bottom.

## What's ours

The orchestration layer, the skill-composition pattern, the `lookup-*` integration design, the rigor pipeline that automatically applies TLP markings, NATO Admiralty source ratings, MISP-scale confidence, and probability-yardstick likelihood language to every product, the [CTI Process Hyperloop](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/cti-process-hyperloop/) framing originally published by Renze Jongman at Mandiant / Google Cloud (2023), and the Liberty91 operational experience that shaped which skills were worth building and how.

## Foundational frameworks and doctrine

- **MITRE ATT&CK** — MITRE Corporation. <https://attack.mitre.org/>
- **Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis** — Sergio Caltagirone, Andrew Pendergast, Christopher Betz (2013).
- **Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain** — Eric Hutchins, Michael Cloppert, Rohan Amin (2011).
- **Pyramid of Pain** — David J. Bianco (2013).
- **F3EAD** — US military targeting doctrine; CTI adaptation popularised by Scott J. Roberts.
- **NATO Admiralty Scale** — NATO source-rating standard, reliability (A–F) × credibility (1–6).
- **ODNI ICD 203 Analytic Standards** and **Sherman Kent's Words of Estimative Probability** — Office of the Director of National Intelligence / CIA.
- **Structured Analytic Techniques for Intelligence Analysis** — Richards J. Heuer Jr. & Randolph H. Pherson (CQ Press / SAGE).
- **Psychology of Intelligence Analysis** — Richards J. Heuer Jr. (CIA Center for Study of Intelligence, 1999).
- **FIRST.org TLP v2.0, CVSS, EPSS** — Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams.
- **CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog** — Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency.
- **UK Government Office for Science** horizon-scanning methodology and Futures Toolkit.
- **US Joint Publication 2-0** — intelligence cycle and Priority Intelligence Requirements doctrine.
- **CMMI** (Carnegie Mellon Software Engineering Institute) — lineage for maturity-level naming.

## Open standards and projects

- **OASIS STIX 2.1 / TAXII** — OASIS Cyber Threat Intelligence Technical Committee.
- **MISP Project** — CIRCL Luxembourg (Andras Iklody, Alexandre Dulaunoy, Christophe Vandeplas, and the wider MISP community).
- **OpenCTI** — Filigran (founders Samuel Hassine and Julien Richard); originated at ANSSI in partnership with CERT-EU.

## A specific credit for the maturity-assessment skill

The 5-level × 6-dimension structure of the `maturity-assessment` skill is an adaptation of Mandiant's [Intelligence Capability Discovery (ICD)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/cti-program-maturity-assessment/) by **John Doyle, Andrew Close, Steven Savoldelli, John Barth, Mark Thomasson and Sachin Kalra** — a CMMI-derived framework with six capability areas and an Initial-to-Adaptive maturity scale. The skill renames the six dimensions for operational clarity, but the structural lineage is theirs. Readers should also know about the separate community framework [CTI-CMM](https://cti-cmm.org/) led by **Michael DeBolt (Intel 471)** and **Colin Connor (IBM X-Force)** with a wider committee of contributors — it uses a different 4-level × 10-stakeholder-domain layout and is well worth comparing to.
