State hackers burrow into water utilities

State-sponsored hackers have broken into water utilities across the region and planted a new backdoor, security firm Northwind said on Thursday.

The findings are set out in a report published by the company.

The campaign has been attributed to Crimson Heron, a group tied to a foreign intelligence service.

“What worries us is how quiet they were. Two of the three utilities had no idea until we told them,” said Dana Reyes, head of incident response at Northwind.

The national cyber agency said in a separate advisory that it had seen scanning for the same vulnerability against government networks.

Attacks on water infrastructure have been rising sharply this year.
